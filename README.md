# Sipwise — PSX Portfolio & SIP

Multi-user investment ledger with D1 persistence, official PSX quote refresh (manual and automatic twice-daily), and sourced Monthly Picks research. Any Google account can sign in; each account gets its own fully isolated portfolio, starting empty. (The original owner's account was seeded from a private CDC statement before this app became multi-user — that seed data is not applied to new accounts.)

## User workflow

1. Sign in with Google. New accounts start with an empty ledger — add holdings via recorded purchases or an imported backup.
2. Record actual purchases (ticker, date, integer shares, price, fees, optional SIP month). Correct an opening entry to add a known average cost. Corrections void the prior entry, retaining its audit record.
3. Record forward stock splits from the company history using the effective date and old/new share ratio. The preview shows the adjusted quantity and average cost before saving; corrections and voids retain the earlier event for audit.
4. PSX prices refresh automatically every 15 minutes during PKT trading hours on weekdays (see `workers/quote-refresh/`); refresh on demand with the "Refresh PSX prices" button, or enter a verified manual quote and date. Every held company and target company needs a price for a complete SIP calculation.
5. In Monthly Picks, select up to 15 saved companies, choose a month and enter fresh investment money. The shortlist is treated as user-approved eligibility.
6. Market Pulse follows that saved shortlist. It uses the owner's pyPSX live feed during estimated market hours when configured, and otherwise reads PSX's five-minute-delayed public data, refreshing once a minute only while the dashboard is visible and the session is open (outside a session it shows the saved closing values). A forced refresh is limited to once a minute per account. pyPSX intraday reads are cached for 30 seconds; the live stream rejects out-of-order ticks, closes after 90 seconds of silence or 30 minutes, and a connected socket alone is never shown as a fresh quote.
7. Request sourced 60–90 day research. It ranks up to five companies, proposes a rupee split, and estimates affordable whole shares from dated PSX quotes. It never records trades automatically.
8. Export a full portfolio backup periodically. This schema is distinct from the company-research dashboard's JSON import; restoring replaces only this ledger and requires confirmation.

## Calculation boundaries

Weighted average cost includes purchase fees. Sales remove shares at the average cost immediately before the sale; missing opening cost keeps cost and return unknown until that position is fully closed or corrected. Same-day entries use ledger order, with opening balances first. Earlier transactions cannot be added before an active opening snapshot; void/correct that snapshot before importing full historical transactions to avoid double-counting.

Cash dividends are tracked in two states. `.github/workflows/psx-payouts.yml` runs `scripts/psx-payout-scrape.mjs` daily after the close and stores each held company's PSX Payouts rows (`https://dps.psx.com.pk/company/payouts`) in the shared `dividend_announcements` table. When the dashboard loads, any cash announcement whose book closure has started becomes an **expected** `PSX auto` dividend: shares held on the entitlement date × per-share amount (PSX quotes dividends as a percentage of face value, default Rs 10, editable per company; amounts printed without a percent sign, e.g. `80(ii) (D)`, are treated the same). Expected dividends are planning figures: they are **not** received income and are excluded from received-income and realised-return totals until you mark them received (optionally entering the actual gross amount and tax withheld) or a matching paid CDC record is imported. The expected amount is recomputed from the ledger, so voiding or correcting a holding never blocks saving. `date` stays the book-closure start; `entitlementDate` and `paymentDate` are kept separately. Automatic entries saved before this change read as expected; nothing is deleted.

Entitlement dates use `lib/psx-calendar.ts`: weekends plus market holidays (the 2026 list is from the published PSX calendar, https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/exchange/general/calendar-holidays; other years know only the fixed national holidays) and the settlement rule in force (T+1 for trades from 9 February 2026, T+2 before). For a year without a complete holiday list the date is flagged unconfirmed. A manual or CDC dividend covers at most one announcement (paired by amount, then date), so a second payout in the same window is still created. A CDC import supersedes an expected record only when the amounts agree and exactly one record matches; an ambiguous match aborts the import with instructions, and the ledger refuses to save an active CDC import alongside an active auto record for the same payout. Voiding an auto record keeps it from returning; correcting one turns it into a manual (received) entry. Bonus shares and right issues are stored but not booked automatically. Apply migrations through `0017_lazy_tenebrous.sql` before deploying.

Tax figures are **estimates** unless a deduction is recorded. Capital gains tax is estimated by netting gains and losses within each Pakistani tax year (1 July – 30 June) and applying the filer or non-filer setting; a broker/NCCPL deduction entered on a sale (`taxWithheld`) and the gross/net amounts from a CDC import are actual figures that are never recalculated when the filer setting changes. Gross gains stay visible when tax is unknown. Real CGT depends on the acquisition date and the rules in force (see https://beta.nccpl.com.pk/cgt), so the estimate is indicative, not a tax liability; a comprehensive tax engine is out of scope.

Market values exclude cash, dividends and unrecorded corporate actions. A recorded forward split multiplies only shares held before its effective date, preserves total cost, and requires a quote dated on or after the split before the holding is valued. Returns are unrealised changes in the remaining holdings, not a total-return or tax report. The estimate allocates integer shares, rounds fee-inclusive unit costs upward to the paisa and remains within the remaining monthly budget. New allocation gaps are capped using 20% of priced holdings plus the remaining contribution. There is no recommendation to sell an existing overweight position. Targets and 183-day screening expiry are editable planning assumptions, not formal Shariah certification.

## AHL statement import and historical dividend sync

**AHL Client Ledger PDF** (Settings, Data & imports, AHL trades; JSON still works). The PDF is read in the browser (`app/research-pdf.ts`, never uploaded) and parsed by `lib/ahl-ledger-pdf.ts`: every row, running balance, page and the printed totals must reconcile or nothing is imported. Only buys and sells become trades; deposits, withdrawals, interest, account charges and standalone tax entries are counted and shown as excluded. The voucher date is the settlement date; the execution date is recovered from the printed `T+1`/`T+2` marker and `lib/psx-calendar.ts` (`executionDateFromSettlement`). The 2026 holiday list is official; the 2025 lunar holidays come from press reports of PSX notices (the exchange publishes only the current year), so a date whose recovery crosses one of those is flagged and must be confirmed in the preview. Gross price is rebuilt from net cash and the disclosed components (the printed rate is fee-inclusive) and snapped to a 0.01 tick only when that reproduces the cash within rounding; averaged fills keep an unrounded price and `fees` absorbs the rounding, so shares × price ± fees equals the reported cash. Identities are `ahlpdf:<account fingerprint>:<voucher>:<symbol>:<side><qty>:<paisa>:<n>` (a truncated SHA-256 of the account code; no name or account number is stored), so re-uploads and overlapping or reordered reports are idempotent while genuinely identical fills stay distinct.

`lib/ahl-reconcile.ts` builds the preview: rows already in the ledger (same statement row, or the same trade from AHL JSON or a manual entry, including one row that equals several execution fills) are skipped; lookalikes and anything that matches another broker's trade need an explicit decision and are never merged automatically; rows previously imported and then voided are skipped unless you re-import them. A sale with no purchase history first uses existing openings and splits, and only the remaining shortfall is assumed to be an acquisition: at the official IPO offer price (curated entries in `lib/ipo-evidence.ts`, or evidence read from PSX offer documents by `scripts/psx-ipo-scrape.mjs`, which you must accept) dated the allotment date, else the listing date; if that cannot be verified, at the gross selling price one calendar day before the sale with zero fees. Assumed acquisitions carry `inferred` provenance, are editable in the preview, and are replaced (fully or partly, old one voided with `supersededBy`) when a later import supplies real purchases.

**Sync dividends** (Settings). `lib/dividend-history.ts` lists the cash announcements your holdings were entitled to over an editable book-closure range (default: first holding to today), including fully sold positions, using split-aware holdings on each cutoff date. The cutoff is the last trade that settles before book closure (T+2 before 9 Feb 2026, T+1 after) on the historical calendar. Announced rupees per share are used as-is; a percentage needs a confirmed face value (never silently Rs 10). Rows with an unconfirmed calendar, an unconfirmed trade date near the cutoff, shares that exist only through an assumed acquisition, or a price break suggesting an unrecorded split/bonus need an explicit acknowledgement. **Approve selected as received** marks them received on your confirmation: the gross entitlement is frozen with its evidence (`Dividend.entitlement`), `paymentDateUnknown` is set, and no tax or net amount is invented. They keep the PSX announcement identity, so the load-time sync neither duplicates nor voids them, existing expected entries are converted in place, voided entries are never offered again, and a later CDC import supersedes the entry (voided, kept for audit). Approval re-plans against the current ledger and announcements and refuses (nothing written) if any selected row changed. Reports count these dividends as received income and list them separately as having no payment date.

Announcement refresh: `POST /api/dividends/refresh` dispatches `psx-payouts.yml` for the ledger's own tickers (a client can narrow but not widen the list), with durable per-company state in `refresh_requests` (queued, running, completed, failed; a completed fetch with 0 rows is a successful empty result; unfinished requests time out after 20 minutes; the first announcement date PSX returned is shown as source coverage). Staging never dispatches these production-writing workflows. IPO evidence uses `GET/POST /api/ipo-offers` and `psx-ipo.yml`. Apply migrations through `0023_special_big_bertha.sql` before deploying.

## Monthly Picks research

Evidence comes from PSX itself, not from an AI web search. For each shortlisted ticker, a GitHub Actions scraper (`scripts/psx-facts-scrape.mjs`, daily after the close plus on demand — PSX blocks Cloudflare egress, so the Worker never fetches PSX itself) fetches and parses `https://dps.psx.com.pk/company/<TICKER>` (`lib/company-facts.ts`) into price, P/E (TTM), 52-week range, four years of annual and four quarters of financials (revenue, profit after taxation, EPS), margin/growth/PEG ratios, and dated announcements — stored per ticker in the shared `company_facts` table (fresh for 7 days; the live `quote_refreshes` price is overlaid so P/E stays current). When a run needs missing or stale tickers the Worker dispatches the workflow (`GITHUB_DISPATCH_TOKEN` secret) and the run waits in a `gathering` step (up to 6 minutes) before ranking; tickers that still have no data are listed as unassessed with the reason. Code then computes deterministic metrics (earnings yield, EPS YoY and CAGR, net-margin trend, 52-week position, most recent announcement age) and a 0–100 quant score per company (`computeMetrics`/`quantScore`/`quantAllocation`) — no AI involvement, fully explainable, and available even if the AI step below is skipped.

One `gpt-5-mini` call (no tools, no web search) then ranks the shortlist using only these code-verified numbers as input, with the quant score as an explicit baseline it may deviate from but must justify. Its output is sanitized in code (`lib/monthly-picks-ai.ts`): unknown or duplicate tickers are dropped, allocations are scaled to total 100% with cash and then held at the 35% per-pick cap by the same `constrainAllocations` rule the quant fallback uses (excess is re-split across the other picks; anything that cannot be placed stays cash), and every shortlisted company gets a coverage entry. If the AI call fails, times out, or its output has nothing usable, the run falls back to `quantResult` — the same result shape built directly from the quant score, with no AI call. A run therefore always finishes as `completed` (`result.method` is `'ai'` or `'quant'`); `failed` is reserved for the case where PSX returned no usable data for any shortlisted company. Source links are attached by code from the fetched PSX page and cited announcement PDFs, not chosen by the model, so there is no citation-validation failure mode.

The AI call, when used, costs roughly $0.005–0.02 per run (a few thousand input tokens, no per-search charges), reserved against a $0.25 per-run cap before it is sent. Each account also has a monthly AI budget (Worker var `AI_MONTHLY_CAP_USD`, default $1 per PKT calendar month, counting all recorded AI usage plus in-flight reserves; unconfirmed, stuck or timed-out calls are charged at their reserve): over it, runs use the quantitative ranking and say why. On-demand company-data scrapes are limited to 10 per account per day and forced price refreshes to one per five minutes (`rate_limits` table). `recommendation_attempts` retains the request, raw response, cost, and any error for that one call. The recommendation row stores an immutable snapshot (fetched facts, computed metrics, quant scores) so a saved result is reproducible without re-fetching PSX. Runs saved by the earlier per-company-evidence workflow remain readable but are never reused as current-workflow evidence; an active row from that workflow is marked `needs_attention` on next load rather than resumed.

Production for this checkout is `https://psx-portfolio-sip.suzairbukhari.workers.dev`. The older ChatGPT Sites publication is a separate deployment, not proof that this Worker has been updated. Apply migrations through `0012_adorable_rocket_racer.sql` before deploying this version.

## Development & deployment

- Node >=22.13; install with npm and preserve package-lock.json.
- Before the first local run, execute `npm run db:migrate:local` once to create the local D1 tables. Then `npm run dev` starts the Vinext preview.
- Local sign-in bypass: put `DEV_AUTH_EMAIL=dev@localhost` (optionally `DEV_AUTH_NAME`) in a git-ignored `.dev.vars` and `npm run dev` treats every request as that user, skipping Google OAuth — no `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`/`SESSION_SECRET` needed locally. `lib/auth.ts` only honours it when the request's `Host` is `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]`, and `.dev.vars` is never deployed, so the deployed Worker still requires a real signed session. Change the email to switch which local portfolio row you load.
- `node --test 'tests/*.test.mjs'` runs all tests: portfolio cost accounting, sales, missing data, dates, monthly budgets, allocation limits and AI weights (`tests/portfolio.test.mjs`); PSX company-page parsing, quant metrics/scoring and allocation (`tests/company-facts.test.mjs`); Monthly Picks AI-output sanitizing and the quant fallback (`tests/monthly-picks-ai.test.mjs`); plus session cookie signing/verification (`tests/session.test.mjs`). The whole suite is portable and runs in CI: the AHL import tests use the synthetic fixture `tests/fixtures/ahl-history.json`, never real broker data.
- `npx tsc --noEmit` checks types; `npm run build` builds the Cloudflare Worker.
- D1 schema is in `db/schema.ts`; generate append-only migrations with `npm run db:generate`. Apply local migrations with `--local --persist-to .wrangler/state`; apply them to your own deployed database with `wrangler d1 migrations apply DB --remote`.
- `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys on every push to `main`: typecheck + tests, remote D1 migrations, build, then both Workers, using the `prod` environment secrets (token needs Workers Scripts:Edit and D1:Edit). The scraper workflows' `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` (also in `prod`) should be a separate token scoped to D1 edit only, since those jobs run on a schedule and on manual dispatch; a failed scrape opens or updates a GitHub issue.
- Staging: every PR from this repo deploys to a separate Worker (`psx-portfolio-sip-staging`) with its own D1 database via `.github/workflows/deploy-staging.yml` (`staging` GitHub environment); merging to `main` still deploys production unchanged. Staging is limited to the owner's Google account, has a lower AI cap, shows a STAGING badge and holds only fictional/public data (`Seed staging` and `Reset staging DB` workflows). One-time setup: `npx wrangler d1 create psx_portfolio_sip_staging` and paste the id into `env.staging` in `wrangler.jsonc`; add `https://psx-portfolio-sip-staging.<subdomain>.workers.dev/api/auth/google/callback` as a Google OAuth redirect URI; create the GitHub `staging` environment (secrets `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`); after the first deploy run `npx wrangler secret put SESSION_SECRET`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` and `OPENAI_API_KEY` with `--env staging`; then run Seed staging. `DEV_AUTH_EMAIL` remains loopback-only and is never used on staging.
- This is a standalone Cloudflare Worker deployment (`wrangler deploy`) in your own Cloudflare account — no external hosting platform is involved. Secrets (`OPENAI_API_KEY`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`, `SESSION_SECRET`, `PYPSX_API_KEY_ID`, `PYPSX_API_SECRET_KEY`, `PYPSX_OWNER_EMAIL`) are set with `wrangler secret put <NAME>`, never committed. Non-secret config (`ALLOWED_EMAILS`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`) lives in `wrangler.jsonc`'s `vars`.
- API requests require a signed-in Google account (any verified account by default; set `ALLOWED_EMAILS` to a comma-separated list to restrict sign-in again); writes also require same-origin requests. Per-user data is isolated with revision checks to reject concurrent stale saves.
- The automatic PSX refresh runs as a second, independent Cloudflare Worker (`workers/quote-refresh/`) on its own Cron Trigger schedule, sharing the same D1 database. Deploy it once with `npm run quote-refresh:deploy`; it writes to a shared `quote_refreshes` cache table that every user's portfolio read merges in, so it never touches per-user save/revision state.
- `quote_refreshes` is the single shared quote cache (`lib/quote-cache.ts`). The cron, "Refresh PSX prices" (`/api/quotes`) and the market pulse (`/api/market-summary`) all read it first and only fetch tickers that are stale (older than 10 minutes while PSX is open; fetched before the last close while it is shut), within a per-request PSX fetch budget that stays under the Cloudflare Workers subrequest cap. A ticker whose fetch fails or is over budget is served from cache and reported as stale. Portfolio reads merge the cache newest-wins, so an older cron row never replaces a newer saved or manual quote.
- PSX has removed its bulk `/market-watch`, `/timeseries/int/*` and `/performers` pages (they return 404). PSX also drops requests from Cloudflare's network (Workers get `520` on every page except the homepage), so live prices are scraped from outside Cloudflare: `.github/workflows/psx-quotes.yml` runs `scripts/psx-quote-scrape.mjs` every 5 minutes during PKT trading hours. One request to `/indices/ALLSHR` prices every All-Share stock (held tickers missing from it, e.g. ETFs, fall back to their company page) and the rows are upserted into `quote_refreshes` through the D1 REST API. The workflow runs in the `prod` GitHub environment and needs its environment secrets `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` and `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` (a token with D1 edit permission); keep `prod` free of required reviewers or wait timers so scheduled runs aren't held for approval. The Workers still try PSX themselves when the cache is older than 10 minutes, but stop at the first `403`/`52x`.
- The optional pyPSX stream accepts only a free paper-account key beginning with `PK_`. `PYPSX_OWNER_EMAIL` must exactly match the signed-in Google account that may use it. The server opens the provider WebSocket and filters messages to that account's saved shortlist; credentials are never returned to browser code. Add the same three values to `.dev.vars` for local verification.

Calculation tests and type/build checks are required. Validate local saving, reload, stale-write rejection, PSX quote refresh, recommendation polling, saved history, and cached-result reuse.

## Run execution, sizing and data freshness

- **Runs finish without a client.** `lib/recommendation-service.ts` holds the Monthly Picks engine. A run row carries a lease token; each step is claimed with a compare-and-set, performed, and released, and every state change is conditional on still holding the lease, so an expired worker cannot finish a newer attempt. The `workers/quote-refresh` Worker has an every-minute Cron Trigger that advances due runs (`processDueRuns`). With `PICKS_BACKGROUND=true` GET `/api/recommendations` is read-only; with it unset or `false` GET also nudges the run under the same lease (rollout/rollback switch). `POST` accepts an `idempotencyKey`; one active run per account; a run older than 30 minutes is failed with a stated reason. Uncertain AI submissions keep their reserved cost and are never resubmitted. Progress (`lib/monthly-picks-progress.ts`) is persisted: phase milestones plus real gathered-company counts, never elapsed time, and only a saved result reaches 100%.
- **Sizing.** The AI (or quant fallback) chooses picks, order, weights and cash. `lib/monthly-picks-allocation.ts` then enforces, in integer paisa, at most 35% of the fresh money per pick and no holding above 20% of (existing portfolio value + fresh money). Overweight holdings get nothing (nothing is sold); constrained money is redistributed pro rata among picks with room, the rest stays cash. A held position with no usable valuation marks the result incomplete instead of counting as zero. Results store `sizing` (limits applied, holdings fingerprint), `versions`, and a frozen snapshot of inputs; a saved run is reused only if inputs and the holdings fingerprint match. Share estimates need a price from the latest completed session.
- **Freshness.** `lib/market-freshness.ts` classifies any observation as fresh, delayed, stale or unavailable using the source time when PSX states it and the trading session otherwise; fetch time alone never makes data fresh, and a failed refresh never refreshes the age of stored data. `/api/quotes`, `/api/price-history` and the four index entries carry this as additive `meta`. Quote writes are ordered by trading day, then source time (`observed_at`), then fetch time, in one conditional upsert shared by the Worker and the scraper (`lib/quote-write.ts`); refresh attempts and failures are recorded separately in `refresh_state`. `pakistanMarketState` honours the holiday calendar and marks years without a published calendar as estimated.
- **Indices and counts.** One PSX homepage fetch yields KSE-100, KSE-30, KMI-30 and All-Share, merged per index so one failure keeps the others (`lib/index-snapshot.ts`); charts for them are sampled from real scrapes, never interpolated. Advance/decline/unchanged counts come from one All-Share table (`lib/market-breadth.ts`) and describe securities in that table, not the number of listed companies. `security_catalog` records the securities PSX lists (no issuer or type is guessed) and only names brand-new placeholder companies.
- **Operations.** `GET /api/admin/health` (super admin) reports active/stuck runs, quote and facts lag, unsettled scrapes and warnings. Staging never dispatches the production-writing facts workflow. GitHub scheduled workflows can be delayed or dropped, so a five-minute schedule is a target, not a guarantee. Users can delete their own holdings data from Settings (`DELETE /api/me/data`).
